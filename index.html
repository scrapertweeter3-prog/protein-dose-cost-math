A 25-gram dose of protein runs 78 cents to $2.37

Protein is bought by the gram long before it is counted by the meal. A $45 whey tub holding 60 scoops at 24 grams each works out to 3.1 cents per gram, so a 25-gram dose costs 78 cents. Chicken breast at $3.49 a pound carries roughly 100 grams of protein per pound, 3.5 cents a gram, 87 cents a dose. A dozen eggs at $4.29 delivers 72 grams, 6 cents a gram, $1.49 a dose. Sirloin at $8.99 a pound yields about 95 grams, 9.5 cents a gram, $2.37 a dose.

Ten dollars spent at each shelf buys a different pile: 320 grams of whey protein, 287 grams of chicken, 168 grams of egg, 106 grams of sirloin. The spread from cheapest shelf to dearest is a touch over threefold, and it survives any plausible sale cycle. The egg and the steak bring their calories and fat along at the same time; the tub is nearly protein alone, which is why its gram is the cheapest gram in the store.

A 100-gram day is four doses. Landed entirely on whey it costs $3.13; landed entirely on sirloin, $9.46; the yearly spread between those two shelves is $2,313. Nobody eats from a single shelf, so the useful number is the swap: one 25-gram sirloin dose replaced by a scoop every day keeps the plate familiar and returns $578 a year, $23,134 across a 40-year training life. The dose reads identical on the label; the ledger behind it does not.
